How to live with Jarvis.

Everything you need, from the first start to writing your own plugins. If something here is wrong or missing, the Report a problem button in his settings goes straight to me.

Install

  1. Download Setup.exe from the latest release and run it. No Python, no command line. Windows 10 or 11.
  2. Get a Claude API key at console.anthropic.com and add a few dollars of credit. That key is his brain; it starts with sk-ant-.
  3. Paste the key when he asks on first start. He checks it before saving and keeps it encrypted on your PC.
  4. Take the tour. It's short: pick his world, try a sentence, learn his voice for you. You can run it again any time: "Jarvis, run the tour again."
First start is slower. He downloads his speech model (about 150 MB) and his voice the first time. The Voice light goes green when he can hear you.

Talking to him

He's always listening, but only acts on sentences that start or end with his name.

Jarvis, open Spotify and skip this song.What's the weather tomorrow, Jarvis?
  • Say just "Jarvis." and he answers. For the next few seconds you don't need the name.
  • Take your time. He waits longer if you sound unfinished.
  • Cut in like you would with a person: start talking and he gets quieter; "wait", "actually" or "hold on" stops him. Esc stops him too.
  • Type instead: click him, press Ctrl+Space, or just start typing in his window.
  • Mute: Ctrl+Alt+M from anywhere, even mid-game. Muting really closes the mic.
  • "Jarvis, close" tucks him into a small corner widget; he keeps listening. "Quit" actually shuts him down.

Answers are short by default. Ask him to "explain" or "walk me through it" when you want the long version.

His desktop

With His world as your desktop on, his world replaces your wallpaper on every monitor and his ring sits in the middle. Desktop icons are hidden while he runs and come back when he quits.

Six worlds

The drafting bench, the orbital station, the archive, the listening post, the cartographer's map and the greenhouse. "Jarvis, switch your desktop to the archive." Each one has live details from your real data: the archive's catalogue counts what he remembers, the map names places after your most-used apps, the greenhouse counts your days together.

What moves

Small things live in each world: stars, dust in the lamplight, rain on the glass, a ship on the map. The light follows your real sunrise and sunset, and it rains or snows when it's actually doing that where you are. It runs at 10 frames a second and stops completely while a game or anything fullscreen is up. Off in performance mode, or with Living scene off.

Widgets and panels

Widgets (system, watchlist, now playing, weather, today, notes, messages) sit in a column; Settings > Arrange the desktop lets you drag them anywhere. While he answers, he pulls panels up beside him: sources he read, a stock, your day, a file he made. "Show me" brings one to the middle.

Orb, screensaver, lights

  • How he looks: the dial, or a soft orb of light.
  • Screensaver: after 5 to 30 idle minutes his world fills the screen with a clock, your next event and the weather. Not during games, videos or fullscreen apps.
  • Lights match his world: switching worlds sets your RGB and smart lights to that world's colour.
Using Wallpaper Engine? It draws over the same layer he does, so you'll see Wallpaper Engine instead of his world. Pause or quit Wallpaper Engine while you use his desktop.

Settings

Click the gear in his window. Press Ctrl+F or / to search every setting by name or description.

  • Most settings apply straight away. Ones marked restart make him restart (a few seconds) when you save, and the save button says so.
  • Default next to a setting puts it back to how he came.
  • You can change almost any of them by voice too: "turn the screensaver off", "use the orb".

What he costs

Jarvis is free. Claude, his brain, is billed by Anthropic to your API key, per use. Short commands cost a fraction of a cent; most people land around a few dollars a month.

  • There's a daily limit (5 dollars to start). He warns you at 80%; at 100% he stops using Claude until tomorrow. Instant commands keep working.
  • "How much have you cost today?" tells you. "Set my budget to 10 dollars" changes it.
  • His numbers are estimates; Anthropic's console has the real bill.

Privacy and what he knows

  • The mic is processed on your PC. Nothing goes to Claude unless you address him.
  • Settings > What he knows lists every single thing he keeps: memories, your slang, birthdays, routines, voiceprints, activity. Change or delete any of it, or wipe a whole section. It all lives in Documents\Jarvis.
  • Screen frames he looks at while you talk stay in memory and are never saved. The camera (glass mode) never leaves your PC.
  • Crash reports are never sent automatically. He fills one in, scrubbed of keys and names, and you decide whether to press Submit.

Your voice and guests

"Learn my voice" and talk for about 20 seconds. After that he answers only you, so the TV and your friends can't set him off.

"Learn my friend Leo's voice" adds a guest. Guests can ask questions, play music and check scores, but can't change settings, touch files, send messages, see your personal things or approve anything. "Forget Leo's voice" removes them.

Gaming

  • He notices when a game starts and goes quiet until you're done, and his desktop stops moving so it costs your game nothing.
  • Post-match stats for Steam games that publish them (add your Steam key and ID; your Steam "Game details" must be public). Valorant with a free HenrikDev key and your Riot ID.
  • "Clip that" presses your recorder's save shortcut (NVIDIA Instant Replay or Xbox Game Bar) and checks a clip really appeared.
  • Use borderless windowed in games. Exclusive fullscreen can flicker when anything else opens.
  • He never clicks or types inside your games.

Plugins

A plugin is one .py file in Documents\Jarvis\plugins. Turn it on in Settings > What he knows > Plugins. There's an example and a README in that folder.

NAME = "Coin flip and dice"
ABOUT = "Flips coins and rolls dice when you ask."

def flip(times=1):
    import random
    times = max(1, min(int(times or 1), 20))
    return ", ".join(random.choice(["heads", "tails"]) for _ in range(times))

TOOLS = [
    {"name": "flip", "description": "Flip a coin. times: how many flips.",
     "params": {"times": "integer"}, "run": flip},
]
  • params types: string, integer, number, boolean.
  • Optional on_start(jarvis) runs once at start; jarvis.say(text) speaks, jarvis.toast(text) shows a note.
  • Claude decides when to use a tool from its description, so write it the way you'd explain it to a person.
Plugins are real code with the same access to your PC as Jarvis. Only turn on ones you trust or have read. A plugin that changes switches off until you approve it again, and one that crashes can't take him down.

Updates

A few seconds after he starts, and every 6 hours, he checks for a new version and downloads it in the background. He tells you when it's ready: "Jarvis, update yourself" restarts into it now, otherwise it installs itself the next time he starts. Your keys, voice, memories and settings are kept outside the program, so updates never touch them. Afterwards he shows what's new since your version.

When something's wrong

He doesn't hear me

Check the Voice light, then Settings > Microphone to pick the right mic. "Run your diagnostics" checks the whole chain and tells you which part failed.

He hears his name when I didn't say it

Learn your voice (above), or raise the mic floor in Settings > Microphone.

My keyboard acts strange, or links download as files

That was a stuck Alt key, fixed in 1.0.35. If it ever happens again, press and release Alt once, then update him.

His desktop doesn't show

Check that Wallpaper Engine (or a similar app) isn't running, and that His world as your desktop is on.

He crashed or won't start

His log is Documents\Jarvis\logs\jarvis.log. Next start he'll offer a crash report; you can also use Settings > General > Report a problem.

Honest limitations

  • Always listening uses a little CPU, since every real sentence is transcribed on your PC to catch his name.
  • His name has to be at the start or end of what you say. "I told my friend about Jarvis" is ignored on purpose.
  • Reminders only fire while he's running; missed ones are announced next start.
  • He can't buy things, trade, or log into your accounts.
  • Echo cancelling works best with headphones or when nothing else is playing through the same speakers.
